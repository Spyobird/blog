Have not written a post in a while so here goes.

Earlier last week I was a bit sick, which made me reflect on the nature of friendships (as I tend to be more philosophical when sick). And in doing so I think I uncovered some gems of the joy and simplicity of friendships, which I would like to share here.

**Chicken soup**

A while ago I came across this concept of a “chicken soup friend” on the internet. It describes a type of friend who is with you through the worst of times, whose presence gives you comfort like a warm bowl of chicken soup. This idea stuck with me because it gives me a cozy and fuzzy satisfaction thinking about it, but I always felt it to be a bit idealistic and impractical in modern day Singapore, where everyone is hustling around.

But while I was sick and feeling the sense of heaviness that comes with malaise, I found what comforted me the most was the most random and unexpected “chicken soup interactions”. These were small check-ins such as an “are you okay?” or a short “gws!” message, from friends some of whom I would not have even expected to reach out. This made me feel seen and cared for despite me probably looking like I wanted to be left alone. I truly appreciate the friends that noticed and were brave enough to reach out.

It also made me wonder whether I have been observant enough to notice others when they are struggling. Or whether I have been courageous enough to reach out to people just for a quick check-in. Have I missed out on any “chicken soup interactions” where I could have comforted someone? I think I can be a little self-focused at times, and so this is a lesson for me to consider others more than myself, for even a little check-in can make someone’s day.

**Friendships minus me**

When I was younger it was easy to wish to have more friends. It was easy to compare myself to people more popular, charming, and loud and to wonder why I was not like them. As an adult I would now see myself as way past that naive way of thinking. And yet the great irony is day in and day out I find myself still striving to this same end, as if maintaining friendships involves a daily dose of humour, telling entertaining anecdotes, initiating activities, and avoiding that thing called awkwardness at all costs.

This daily "recital of friendship" stems from too big a focus on the "me", meaning my ego and self-consciousness. This realisation hit me one day when I discovered how much I enjoyed friendships more if I did not care about what the other person thought about me. Which led me to also notice that I many times care more about how I am perceived, rather than about the other person. The pursuit of friendship is not to boost my ego, neither is it a reflection of my popularity or charisma, but it is about genuine connections with another human being.

The most humbling joy in friendships is realising I'm nowhere near the funniest, coolest, most charismatic, smartest, most attractive, kindest, or whatever other quality there might be, but yet there are a non-zero number of people that choose to connect, to show care, to be loyal, to enjoy my presence, to call me a friend. And so I'm also learning to let go of the "me" in friendships. To find that quiet assurance. To see the other person for who they are. To show up for someone else. To stop thinking about what I want, and start thinking of what someone else might need.

I've heard this phrase go around “be interested, not interesting". I think the core truth behind that is about letting go of the need to impress others, and instead to focus on being present with someone else. And that has shaped a sizable part of my philosophy of friendships. I believe everyone around me is on their own unique, complex journey. Everyone has a story to tell, and it is a joy, opportunity and privilege to discover that as a friend.

**The treasure called serendipity**

When I look back at my own friendships, I'm starting to see more and more that they are serendipitous. Friendships are not chosen—they happen because we cross paths with someone else. I think that makes the friendships we have ever more precious, because we will never know when these paths may uncross. This helps me to appreciate the small joys of friendship that often go overlooked, such as everyday conversations, inside jokes, or shared memories.

For me one of the hardest things to do is dropping a mindset of "like vs dislike". What I mean by this is that it is easy to hang out with people I enjoy being around, and in some sense it is only natural to. But I'm learning to extend the same warmth and curiosity to others that are in my orbit. Honestly, life is too short to be overly selective about friends, and I often find the most unlikely of interactions can spark the closest friendships. It's easy to stay within one's comfort zone, and reaching out takes real effort, but it is often worth it.

This is still a work in progress. I'm still navigating the complexities of friendships each day. But I think being forced to slow down helped me to focus on what I truly value, and what brings me energy, which are really the simple things. And I hope that this will help me show up more for the people around me. :)
